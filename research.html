Research

Research themes, articles, talks and seminars

My research sits at the intersection of gravitation, black-hole physics, quantum field theory, holography and quantum information. A recurring theme is the search for structures that remain simple or universal even when one moves beyond the most familiar theories.

Black holes

Black-hole solutions and dynamics, regular geometries, collapse, interiors, extremality and singularities.

Higher-curvature gravity

Higher-derivative extensions of General Relativity, especially Einsteinian cubic and generalized quasi-topological gravities.

Entanglement in quantum field theory

Universal entanglement structures, mutual information, reflected entropy and conformal constraints.

Holography and quantum gravity

Holographic entanglement, quantum aspects of spacetime, generalized symmetries and related geometric questions.

Black holes

A substantial part of my work concerns the structure and dynamics of black holes, particularly when the gravitational dynamics differs from Einstein’s theory. Topics include regular black holes, gravitational collapse, the internal structure of black holes, extremal and near-extremal limits, echoes and compact objects, and the role of higher-curvature corrections in singularity resolution.

Higher-curvature gravity

I have worked extensively on Einsteinian cubic gravity and on the broader families of generalized quasi-topological gravities. These theories contain higher powers of the curvature while retaining unusually simple equations in physically important sectors. This makes it possible to investigate black holes, cosmology, holography and effective-field-theory questions in settings much richer than General Relativity but still analytically tractable.

Entanglement in quantum field theory

A second major line of work uses entanglement and information-theoretic quantities to probe the structure of quantum field theories. This includes universal contributions to entanglement entropy, singular and deformed entangling surfaces, mutual and multipartite information, reflected entropy, and bounds on CFT data derived from entanglement constraints.

Holography and quantum gravity

Holography provides a bridge between these directions. I have used gravitational theories to study entanglement and CFT observables, and conversely quantum-information ideas to constrain gravitational dynamics. Related work concerns generalized symmetries, holographic entanglement entropy, geometric functionals and aspects of quantum gravity.
